How to Choose a Digital Marketing Agency in Ahmedabad

What to expect, which questions to ask, which promises to distrust and how to compare proposals fairly.

By Hitesh Rathod · 7 min read

Searching for a digital marketing agency in Ahmedabad returns a long list of companies that all promise more visibility, more leads and more growth. The difficulty is not finding an agency. It is telling which one fits your business and will be honest with you. This guide gives you a simple way to decide.

A quick disclosure: We Are Amdavadi is a digital marketing agency, so we have an interest in this topic. We have written the guide so that it is useful whichever agency you choose, and we suggest you hold us to the same questions.

Start with your goal, not the service list

Before speaking to any agency, write down what you want in one or two sentences. “More enquiries for our clinic from people living within 10 km” is a goal an agency can work with. “Do SEO and social media” is a task list, not a goal.

It also helps to know your basics: what an enquiry is worth, how enquiries reach you today (phone, WhatsApp, form, walk-ins) and who answers them. Marketing that brings enquiries to a phone nobody answers will not pay for itself.

What a digital marketing agency should actually cover

Services overlap, so ask which ones you really need right now:

  • SEO helps your website appear in unpaid Google results. It is a steady, longer-term channel. Read our overview of SEO services.
  • Local SEO focuses on Google Maps and local searches, which matters most for businesses that serve customers in a particular area. See local SEO.
  • Google Ads places paid ads in front of people searching right now. It can start quickly but stops when the budget stops. See Google Ads management.
  • Social media marketing builds familiarity and trust over time, and works best when there is a clear plan and consistent content.
  • Website design matters because every channel sends people to your site. A slow, confusing site wastes the traffic you pay for or work for.

A good agency will not try to sell all of these. It will tell you which one or two to do first, and why.

Freelancer, large agency or founder-led

Each model has trade-offs, and none is automatically better.

OptionStrengthsWatch out for
Individual freelancerDirect contact, flexible, often lower cost.Single point of failure if they are unavailable. Check how they handle holidays and handover.
Larger agencySeveral specialists and established processes.You may deal with account managers rather than the people doing the work. Ask who will actually work on your account.
Founder-led agencyThe person planning your strategy is the person accountable for it.Limited capacity. Ask how many clients they take on and what they will not do.

Questions to ask before you hire

  1. What would you do first for my business, and why? A thoughtful answer refers to your situation, not a generic package.
  2. Who will do the work? Ask for a name and whether any work is outsourced.
  3. How will you measure progress? Look for enquiries, calls, form submissions and WhatsApp messages, not only impressions or followers.
  4. What reports will I receive, and how often? Ask to see a sample structure.
  5. What do you need from me? Access, content, approvals and quick answers all affect results.
  6. Who owns the accounts? Your Google Business Profile, ad account, analytics and website should be in your name.
  7. What happens if we part ways? You should be able to leave with your accounts and assets intact.

Red flags to avoid

  • Guaranteed rankings or guaranteed leads. Nobody controls Google’s results. Google’s own guidance warns that no one can guarantee a top ranking.
  • Bought reviews or fake testimonials. They can get a business profile penalised and they damage trust.
  • Mass low-quality links. Cheap link packages can do more harm than good.
  • No access to your own accounts. If the agency keeps your data in its own accounts, you are locked in.
  • Vague reports full of jargon. You should understand what was done and what happened as a result.
  • Pressure to sign immediately. A good fit survives a few days of consideration.

How to compare proposals

When you have two or three proposals, put them side by side and check:

  • Is the scope specific (for example, number of pages optimised, campaigns set up, posts per month) or vague?
  • Is it clear what is paid to the agency and what is paid to platforms, such as ad spend?
  • Does each proposal start from your goal?
  • Are timelines described as ranges and expectations, rather than promises?
  • Do you understand every line without needing a translator?

What a sensible first three months looks like

Exact plans differ, but a reasonable start usually includes understanding your business and customers, reviewing your current website and online presence, agreeing priorities, fixing the foundations (such as your Google Business Profile, tracking and key pages), and then starting regular work with a simple monthly review. Early months are mostly about foundations. Be cautious of anyone who promises dramatic results in the first few weeks.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I budget for digital marketing?

There is no single right number. Start from what an enquiry or customer is worth to your business and how many you need, then decide what you can invest steadily for at least a few months. Be wary of anyone who quotes a price before understanding your business, and remember that Google Ads spend is paid to Google on top of an agency’s management fee.

Should I just choose the cheapest agency?

Price matters, but the cheapest proposal often leaves out reporting, tracking or hands-on work. Compare what is included, who does the work and how results will be reported, not only the monthly fee.

Can I ask to see client results?

Yes. A fair request is to ask how the agency measures progress and to see how a report is structured. Newer agencies may not have shareable case studies, so ask them to explain their process and what they would measure for you instead.

Do I need a long contract?

Not necessarily. Digital marketing takes time, but you should still understand the minimum term, how to end the arrangement and who owns the accounts, content and website access at the end.
